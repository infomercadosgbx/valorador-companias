Frontier AI can identify and weaponize a software exploit in minutes while most enterprises still validate their defenses on a quarterly schedule.

In part three of his Frontier AI series, Broadcom CIO Alan Davidson shares why speed is now the defining security challenge, and why periodic penetration testing is an artifact of a slower era.

As Broadcom accelerates its own release and patch cycles, Alan's team is shifting to daily, continuous testing, using white-hat models to probe environments in real time as workloads move into production.

His core operating philosophy for CIOs navigating machine-speed threats: "I'd rather be down than breached."

Watch Alan break down how continuous validation works in practice, and explore his 4 steps for accelerating enterprise defenses safely in the full article (link in the comments).

When you're scaling AI clusters past 100,000 XPUs, the network can't be the bottleneck.

Meet Tomahawk 6 – Davisson, the industry's first 102.4-Tbps Ethernet switch with Co-Packaged Optics (CPO), first unveiled in October 2025 to solve the hardest power and reliability challenges in AI networking.

By bringing 16 × 6.4 Tbps optical engines directly onto the switch package, we've re-engineered how data moves across massive AI fabrics:

➡️Slash optical power by 70% (over 3.5x more efficient than traditional pluggables).
➡️Maximize GPU/XPU uptime with rock-solid link stability that minimizes link flaps during long training runs.
➡️Scale effortlessly from 512-XPU scale-up domains to 100,000+ XPU scale-out clusters at 200 Gbps per channel.
➡️Maintain operational flexibility with field-replaceable external laser modules and open IEEE 802.3 interoperability.

More bandwidth. Less power. Higher cluster utilization. That is how AI infrastructure scales.
